# Financial Health Scanner: scoring engine

This engine turns raw survey answers into four dimension scores (0–100), a predicted score on the CFPB well-being scale, age-band percentiles, and a habits score that is shown but not scored. The model is fitted on the CFPB National Financial Well-Being Survey 2016 public-use file (PUF), using the survey weight `finalwt`.

`score.py` (Python standard library) and `score.js` (no dependencies, browser or Node) give the same output for the same input. Both match the analysis to within 1e-13 for all 6,394 PUF respondents.

## Files
| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `score.py` | Python engine: `score(answers)`, `what_if(baseline, change)` |
| `score.js` | JS engine: `score(answers)`, `whatIf(baseline, change)`. In a browser it is `window.FHS`; in Node, `require("./score.js")`. **Generated; edit `score.template.js` instead.** |
| `model_params.json` | Coefficients, item coding, age bands and percentile tables. Generated by `../fwb_analysis.py`. |
| `test_cases.json`, `test_score.py`, `test_score.js` | 5 scoring cases and 1 what-if case, with expected outputs |
| `make_test_cases.py` | Regenerates `test_cases.json` (only after the model changes) |
| `validate_against_puf.py` / `.js` | Checks both engines against every PUF respondent |

## Model (`outputs/final_model.json`)
The prediction is `predicted = 16.4408 + 0.18319·reserves + 0.24150·confidence_skill + 0.10623·debt + 0.02929·planning_protection`, clipped to 14–95. The clip never binds in practice.

| Dimension | Raw coef [95% CI] | Std. β [95% CI] | Share of the score's weight* |
|---|---|---|---|
| reserves | 0.1832 [0.168, 0.198] | 0.441 [0.405, 0.477] | 46.6% |
| confidence_skill | 0.2415 [0.218, 0.265] | 0.311 [0.281, 0.341] | 32.9% |
| debt | 0.1062 [0.085, 0.128] | 0.133 [0.106, 0.161] | 14.1% |
| planning_protection | 0.0293 [0.017, 0.042] | 0.060 [0.034, 0.087] | 6.4% |

- **Fit:** weighted R² 0.572, 5-fold CV R² 0.572, n = 5,208. The sample is everyone with a valid score, all four dimensions, and an answer to SAVINGSRANGES.
- **Mean absolute error vs the actual FWBscore:** 7.2 points (unweighted).
- *These shares are the standardized betas rescaled to sum to 100%. They are for communication only; the engine uses the raw coefficients.
- **Robust standard errors (HC1)**, with `finalwt` as a probability weight.
- **Association, not causation:** the data is one survey at one point in time.

## Output of `score(answers)`
Test case 1 (a 30-year-old), rounded here for readability; the engine returns full precision:
```json
{
  "age_band": "25-34",
  "dimensions": {
    "reserves":            {"score": 50.0,  "percentile": 41.5},
    "confidence_skill":    {"score": 56.04, "percentile": 31.3},
    "debt":                {"score": 100.0, "percentile": 77.3},
    "planning_protection": {"score": 41.67, "percentile": 60.3}
  },
  "predicted_score": 50.98,
  "predicted_score_clipped": false,
  "predicted_score_percentile": 43.8,
  "missing_dimensions": [],
  "habits_display_only": {"score": 60.63, "percentile": 46.8},
  "warnings": []
}
```
The debt score of 100 sits at only the 77th percentile because many peers also score 100. Under the mid-rank rule a tie counts as half above and half below, so a perfect debt score never shows as the 99th percentile.

`what_if(baseline, change)` returns:
- `baseline_score`, `new_score` and `predicted_gain` (the change in predicted points)
- `dimension_changes` (change in each dimension score)
- `baseline_percentile` and `new_percentile`

For example, raising SAVINGSRANGES from 3 to 5 in test case 1 moves reserves up 16.7 points and gives a predicted gain of **+3.05**.

## Scoring rules
1. **Item rescaling.** Each valid answer is rescaled to 0–1 as (code − lowest code) / (highest code − lowest code). Reverse-coded items (marked R below) are flipped: 1 − value.
2. **Missing answers.** Null, blank, −1 (refused), and the item's own "don't know" or "prefer not to say" codes all count as missing. Any other unexpected code also counts as missing and adds a warning.
3. **Financial skill composite.** The 10 FS items (FS2_3 reversed) are averaged into one item, `FS_items_mean`. It needs at least 5 of the 10 answered, and counts as **one** of the 4 confidence items.
4. **Dimension score.** A dimension is 100 × the mean of its valid items. It is scored only if at least half its items are valid:
   - reserves: at least 1 of 2
   - confidence_skill: at least 2 of 4
   - debt: at least 3 of 6
   - planning_protection: at least 3 of 6
   - habits: at least 4 of 8
5. **Predicted score.** It is computed only when all four scored dimensions are present. Otherwise it is `null` and `missing_dimensions` lists the gaps.
6. **Percentiles.** Each percentile is the weighted share of PUF respondents in the same age band who score below the value, plus half the share who score the same (ties split evenly). Values are rounded half-up to whole numbers first.
   - Dimension percentiles compare against that dimension's distribution in the band.
   - `predicted_score_percentile` compares against peers' **predicted** scores from the same model. The peers are PUF respondents with all 4 dimensions and SAVINGSRANGES answered (n = 5,209); the actual FWBscore is not used. So a client is ranked on the same compressed scale the engine produces.
7. **Age band.** The engine uses `agecat` (CFPB codes 1–8) if given, otherwise `age` in years. Bands are 18–24, 25–34, 35–44, 45–54, 55–61 and 62+ (agecat 6–8). Under 18, or no age, gives `null` percentiles.

## Assumptions and caveats
1. **Two items use the PUF's codes, not the questionnaire's.**
   - SAVINGSRANGES: the questionnaire offered 13 bands; the PUF collapses them to 7. The app must offer the 7 PUF bands.
   - MANAGE1: the questionnaire had 6 options with a separate "Not applicable"; the PUF merges it with "Never" as code 1, scored as the worst value. A client with no bills is therefore scored like one who never pays on time. Consider adding "N/A" in the app and passing null.
2. **The engine is fitted only on people who answered SAVINGSRANGES.**
   - Without SAVINGSRANGES, R² drops from 0.572 to 0.313 on that group. The engine still scores those clients but adds a warning, so make SAVINGSRANGES required.
   - Fitting on everyone instead would give R² 0.545 (see `outputs/model_variants.csv`).
3. **The predicted score has a ceiling of 72.5.** That is the prediction for a client at 100 on all four dimensions; the floor (all four at 0) is 16.4.
   - Regression pulls predictions toward the average, so the predicted score can't reach the top of the real scale. Among 62+ peers, the 90th percentile of actual scores is 79, but of predicted scores only 67.2.
   - Strong Next Gen clients will cluster near the ceiling. Call the score a "CFPB-scale estimate".
4. **Score percentiles are ranked against peers' predicted scores.**
   - A perfect profile now ranks at the 99.8th percentile (62+), instead of the 81st when ranked against actual scores.
   - The trade-off: a percentile no longer means "X% of real people your age score lower on the CFPB scale". It means "X% of peers would get a lower estimate from this scanner".
   - Don't show the predicted score next to the CFPB's published score distribution: the two use different scales.
   - Peers' actual scores (FWBscore) and predicted scores both appear in `outputs/benchmarks_by_age.csv`.
5. **Checkbox items (PRODHAVE, PRODUSE).** Unticked means 0. If the question wasn't shown, pass null, not 0.
6. **AUTOMATED "I do not have this type of account" (7)** is scored as 0 (not automating), as in the analysis.
7. **What-if gains** hold every other answer fixed and are model associations, not promised effects. Gains are capped by the item ranges. If a change makes a dimension missing, the gain is null.
8. **Data limits.** US data from 2016, self-reported, one point in time. The top income band is $150k+ and the top savings band $75k+, so wealthy clients are under-represented.
